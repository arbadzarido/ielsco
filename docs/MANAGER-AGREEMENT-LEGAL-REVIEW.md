# Manager Offering Letter & Engagement Agreement: legal-review checklist

**Status: DRAFT, PENDING LEGAL REVIEW.** This is a working checklist for the Founder and a qualified Indonesian lawyer. It is not legal advice and reaches **no legal conclusion**. Nothing here was verified against the official text of any law; the points about the 2026 law come from news and law-firm articles and must be checked against the promulgated text (JDIH / Setneg).

## A. Facts to establish first

1. **The real arrangement.** Who sets the work and the hours, is there a monthly payment for work, for how long, are the managers free to work elsewhere, can they be replaced by someone else? The brief describes a monthly payment, expected hours (about 12 a week), a weekly meeting, a defined role and accountability to the founders. These are features lawyers weigh when deciding whether a relationship is employment, whatever it is called.
2. **Which company contracts.** The database holds two separate legal entities: IELSco = PT English Space Berkah Indonesia, Elco AI = PT Elco Edukasi Berkah Indonesia. The brief says both operate under PT English Space Berkah Indonesia. Confirm whether Elco AI managers contract with PT English Space (then the template needs a fixed party) or with PT Elco Edukasi (the template names the chosen company's entity, as built).

## C. Checklist for the Founder and the lawyer

Mark each item **Resolved / Change the text / Change the structure**.

1. **Legal nature.** Employment (fixed-term), internship, partnership / service agreement, volunteer, or another form. Record the reasoning, because the label "financial appreciation" does not decide it.
2. **If employment.** PKWT form and Indonesian-language requirement; whether registration with the labour office applies; minimum-wage compliance for the amounts; BPJS Kesehatan and Ketenagakerjaan enrolment; income-tax (PPh 21) withholding; religious-holiday allowance (THR) and leave pro rata; end-of-term compensation; the early-termination consequences.
3. **If internship.** Whether the program meets the formal requirements (program, mentor, partner institution, allowance, insurance) and the 6-month cap if the new law applies.
4. **If a service / partnership agreement.** Real independence, deliverables-based pay, and the risk of being treated as disguised employment given the hours and meeting expectations.
5. **If the amounts do not fit the chosen form.** Do not change the amounts in the template. Record the options with their cost (for example higher pay for fewer paid hours, a formal internship with an allowance, or a project-based arrangement) and let the founders decide within their real budget.
6. **Probation.** Confirm the Phase 1 evaluation cannot operate as a probation clause. Confirm clause 12 (review) is acceptable for a fixed term.
7. **Working time (clause 3).** Whether "about 12 hours a week" and "flexible hours" are acceptable as drafted; whether any hours record is needed.
8. **Payment (clause 5).** The wording that calls the amount an appreciation but says mandatory rules apply regardless. Tax and social-security treatment.
9. **Additional incentives (clause 6).** If the relationship is employment, whether commissions and profit shares count as wages. The template sets no amounts or percentages by design.
10. **Opportunities (clause 7).** That items 7.3 to 7.6 are phrased as intentions or opportunities and create no enforceable promise: future business-unit CEO role, the 2027 overseas program (Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, July–August 2027, funded travel "intended"), and the external SGD 600–900 internships. Tax and visa consequences of funded travel.
11. **Confidentiality (clause 8).** Two-year tail, personal-data duties (the personal data protection law), carve-outs.
12. **Intellectual property (clause 9).** Assignment of work product to the Company, the Manager's portfolio right, moral rights.
13. **Conflict of interest (clause 10).** That it does not unlawfully limit the Manager's other work.
14. **Termination (clause 13).** The 14-day notice is only a **proposal**. Notice periods, grounds, and any severance or remaining-term payment rights.
15. **Dispute resolution (clause 15).** Forum and any mandatory industrial-relations procedure.
16. **Language.** An Indonesian-language version (or bilingual text) if an Indonesian party is involved.
17. **Parties and signing.** The correct legal entity, authorised signatory, whether the signatory's authority is documented, and electronic-signature validity.
18. **Minors and students.** Age of the managers (adult capacity) and any university rules on outside work.
19. **Record keeping.** Where signed copies are kept and who may read them. The register already limits readers to founders, named People personnel and the manager concerned (once approved).

## D. Before marking it legally approved

The template stays "Draft, pending legal review" until a founder, after the lawyer's sign-off, ticks the confirmation in Admin → Document templates. That is recorded with the founder's name and the text revision. Changing the wording later (a code change that raises the revision) cancels the approval for the new wording. Until then, every generated document carries a draft banner and drafting notes, and cannot be marked Approved.
